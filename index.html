Privacy policy — Scoutly

Last updated: November 1, 2026 · Applies to Scoutly version 5.6.0

Scoutly is a Chrome extension for researching products listed on supported Amazon retail marketplaces. This policy describes the information Scoutly processes and how it is used. Scoutly is not affiliated with or endorsed by Amazon.

What Scoutly does in one line

Scoutly's single purpose: to help sellers research Amazon products — reading listing information, building sales and profit estimates, and tracking products over time. Sales and revenue figures are estimates derived from public page signals; they are not actual seller order records.

Information Scoutly reads

When you use Scoutly, it reads information displayed on Amazon product and search-result pages that you open or that you ask Scoutly to look up. Depending on the page, this may include:

  • product names, brands, ASINs, product images and links;
  • prices and currencies;
  • ratings, review counts, and Amazon sales badges;
  • Best Sellers Rank (BSR);
  • availability, seller counts, and general offer information.

Keyword research can process the keyword you enter and Amazon's suggested search terms. The profit calculator stores the values you enter, such as product cost, shipping, and fees. The tracker stores observations of the products you track and your tracker settings.

Scoutly uses these details to show product research, calculate sales and revenue estimates, calculate potential profit, maintain tracking history, and provide exports and backups.

Where your data is kept

Everything Scoutly stores stays on your device. Scoutly has no analytics, no advertising, no user accounts, and it does not sell, rent, or share your data. Nothing is sent to the developer — Scoutly does not have a server.

Scoutly keeps data in three places inside your browser:

  1. Chrome's local extension storage (chrome.storage.local) — stored until you delete it or uninstall: tracker products and their history, your settings (including scheduled-check preferences and stop words), recent lookups (capped at 20 entries), saved keyword research and its caches, calculator inputs and drafts, the exchange-rate cache, and sales-estimate calibration data used to keep estimates consistent for products you revisit.
  2. Chrome's temporary session storage (chrome.storage.session) — used only while Chrome is open (for example: which of Scoutly's single-purpose reader windows are running, quick-view usage throttles, and sourcing tasks). Chrome clears it automatically when the browser closes; it is never synced or sent anywhere.
  3. Your browser's local storage — one small preference: which X-Ray columns you have hidden on the Product Research page.

Retention

Tracker history keeps full detail for the last 30 days; older snapshots are thinned to one snapshot per day, and the whole history is capped at 1,500 entries per product. Quick-view product caches expire after 1 day. Recent lookups keep the 20 latest entries. Data in session storage is cleared automatically whenever you close Chrome.

Deleting your data

Use Settings → Data & storage → Clear all tracker data in Scoutly to delete tracked products and history (Scoutly first offers to save a backup file). Clearing extension data in Chrome, or uninstalling Scoutly, removes all extension storage on your device. Deleting your browser's local storage removes the X-Ray column preference.

Exports and backups

Exports (CSV or JSON) are created in your browser and saved to files in the location you choose. Scoutly does not transmit them anywhere. Exported files contain the tracked data shown in the extension; they are yours — keep them safe.

Scoutly may open a temporary, single-purpose Chrome window to read an Amazon page (for example, for a single-product lookup, an X-Ray scan, a scheduled tracker check, or an automatic quick-view read). The window is closed automatically right after the read. Quick-view reads are deliberately throttled (small delays between reads, a rolling limit per 10 minutes, and a per-product cooldown).

Scheduled checks and notifications

Scheduled tracker checks are off by default. If you enable them (Settings → Tracker → Background checks), Chrome reads your tracked product pages on the interval you choose (every 3–24 hours) while Chrome is open, so history continues building. Notifications are created locally by Chrome when a check finds a price or rank alert; they are never sent anywhere and contain alert text only.

Security

Because Scoutly processes and keeps data locally in your browser, data does not pass through any Scoutly-operated server. Protect access to your Chrome profile and any exported backup files.

Children

Scoutly is not designed for or directed to children, and Scoutly does not knowingly collect information from children.

Limited Use

Scoutly's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs, if any, adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Scoutly does not use or transfer user data outside the purposes disclosed here, does not sell user data, does not use it for advertising, and does not allow any human to read it. Scoutly does not collect or transmit personal user data to its developer.

Permissions Scoutly requests, in plain words

  • storage — keeps all the data described above on this device.
  • alarms — runs the optional scheduled tracker refresh (off by default).
  • notifications — shows locally generated desktop notifications for tracker alerts.
  • Amazon hosts (www.amazon.* and completion.amazon.*) — read listing pages and keyword suggestions for the marketplaces Scoutly supports.
  • cdn.jsdelivr.net — exchange-rate data for currency conversion.
  • *.alibaba.com — the "find suppliers" action.
  • Amazon image hosts — pick up the product image for the Alibaba image search.
